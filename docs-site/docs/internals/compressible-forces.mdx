## What it is

The faithful solver's pressure/force family converts normalized incompressible panel gamma to pressure coefficient at the requested freestream Mach. It integrates that pressure on the existing contour for CL and quarter-chord CM. Total wake drag uses physical edge speed and density. The separate CDf and CD−CDf drag fields use XFOIL CDCALC wall-shear projection and have bounded reference coverage. Python/CLI coefficient results, trial operating corrections and the shipping WASM Cp array use the same conventions. Coordinates and thicknesses are normalized by chord; gamma and U are normalized by freestream speed. Angles passed to force integration are radians.

## Theory

The pressure correction is XFOIL CPCALC/CLCALC, not just Prandtl–Glauert scaling:

$$
C_{p,0}=1-\gamma^2,\quad C_p=\frac{C_{p,0}}{\beta+\frac{M_\infty^2}{2(1+\beta)}C_{p,0}},\quad \beta=\sqrt{1-M_\infty^2}\label{eq:compressible-forces-1}
$$

Equation $\eqref{eq:compressible-forces-1}$ defines this relation.

The pressure array is integrated by the existing panel force routine; its Mach-zero wrapper preserves the old public calling convention. XFOIL CDCALC applies the Squire–Young wake extrapolation using the physical wake speed, with wake shape factor formed from total displacement thickness:

$$
C_D=2\theta_w\left(\frac{U_w}{U_\infty}\right)^{(5+H_w)/2},\qquad C_{f,\infty}=C_{f,e}\frac{\rho_e}{\rho_\infty}\left(\frac{U_e}{U_\infty}\right)^2\label{eq:compressible-forces-2}
$$

Equation $\eqref{eq:compressible-forces-2}$ defines this relation.

Source: [official XFOIL 6.99](https://web.mit.edu/drela/Public/web/xfoil/), xfoil.f CPCALC/CLCALC/CDCALC. This is the same approximate aerodynamic model used by the reference, not evidence of post-stall or transonic physical validity.

## Why and limits

Reusing one CPCALC helper prevents WASM's Cp display and the faithful solver's reported forces from drifting apart. Carry Mach into trial CL calculations as well as final reporting, so operating corrections use the same pressure model. Preserve the old Mach-zero public force function for existing Rust callers; add an explicit Mach variant instead of silently changing its input contract.

The change retains existing contour integration and canonical wake/friction routines. The full reference gate checks integrated CL/CD/CM, all WASM surface Cp values and binding agreement on the bounded matrix. Other Reynolds/Mach scaling modes and arbitrary geometry integration are not established by those cases. The reference viscosity-ratio limitation is documented in [compressible transport](./compressible-boundary-layer). Physical-model validity requires independent experimental evidence; a converged solve alone is insufficient.

The reference gate now checks total CD, `cd_friction`, and `cd_pressure` on all 48 cases. `cd_pressure` means **CD−CDf**, matching XFOIL's iteration report. The polar-file CDp column is a distinct surface-pressure integral from CLCALC and is not this field. No new surface-pressure-drag output is added.

CDCALC integrates local wall shear along the freestream direction, beginning with the first pair of real surface stations (IBL=3 in Fortran), excluding the synthetic stagnation row and wake:

$$
C_{Df}=\sum_{IS=1}^{2}\sum_{IBL=3}^{IBLTE(IS)}\frac{(\tau_{IBL} + \tau_{IBL-1})}{\rho_\infty U_\infty^2}\left[\Delta x\cos\alpha+\Delta y\sin\alpha\right],\qquad \tau=\frac12\rho_e U_e^2 C_{f,e}\label{eq:compressible-friction}
$$

Equation $\eqref{eq:compressible-friction}$ keeps the sign of wall shear and projection and includes low-Reynolds-number surface stations. The canonical force bridge carries physical speed, density-weighted Cf and actual surface y coordinates. Legacy clamp/low-Rtheta exclusions and absolute-x projection are removed from this canonical route; the separate legacy station-based engine remains unvalidated.
